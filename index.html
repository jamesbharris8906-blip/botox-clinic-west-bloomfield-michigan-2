Botox is a purified protein that relaxes the muscles that fold the skin into lines. A few small injections go into the muscles that pull the brows together, raise the forehead, and crinkle the corners of the eyes. Lasers & Skin Rejuvenation

Lasers & Skin Rejuvenation treats the skin itself, not the muscle under it. Light energy warms the deeper layers and prompts the skin to make fresh collagen, which keeps skin firm and smooth. It helps with sun spots, uneven tone, fine lines, and the rough texture that Michigan winters and summer sun leave behind. A session takes less than an hour, the skin may look pink for a day or two, and the change builds over weeks. People who pair laser work with Botox often find the two cover different ground: Botox softens the lines that movement makes, and the laser works on the tone and texture that movement has nothing to do with.

Microneedling Facials & Peels

Microneedling Facials & Peels work by giving the skin a reason to repair itself. Tiny needles make small channels in the skin, and a peel or a serum goes in after them, reaching layers a cream on the surface never touches. The skin responds by making collagen and turning over fresh cells, which softens fine lines, fades old acne marks, and smooths rough patches. It is a good choice for someone who wants real change without a long recovery, and it pairs well with Botox because the two treat different layers: one relaxes the muscle, the other rebuilds the surface.

Injectables & PRF

Injectables & PRF cover the treatments that add volume or use the body's own growth factors to refresh the skin. Fillers restore volume in cheeks and lips and soften deep folds, while PRF uses a small sample of your own blood, spun down to concentrate the platelets, and places those growth factors where the skin needs repair. PRF is a natural fit for people who like the idea of their own body doing the work, and it is often paired with microneedling so the growth factors reach deep into the skin. Botox and fillers are often confused, but they do different jobs: Botox relaxes a muscle, and a filler fills a space. A plan that uses both, in the right amounts, looks far more natural than either one used alone.

Body Contouring & Wellness

Body Contouring & Wellness looks at the body the way Botox looks at the face: small, planned changes that add up. Non-surgical contouring treatments target stubborn areas that diet and exercise have not moved, while wellness care looks at the habits that shape how the skin ages, from sleep to stress to hydration. No single session reshapes a body, and any clinic that promises that is selling a story, not a treatment. The honest version is a series of visits with clear goals, measured progress, and a plan that fits a real schedule.

XERF Skin Tightening

XERF Skin Tightening uses radiofrequency energy to warm the deeper layers of the skin without breaking the surface. The heat prompts the skin to tighten and make new collagen, which helps with loose skin along the jaw, the neck, and the cheeks. There is no cutting and no downtime worth planning around, which is why people who are not ready for surgery ask about it first. Results build slowly over a few months, and most people need a series of sessions to get the full effect. It is a steady, quiet kind of change, and it suits people who want to look rested rather than different.

Eyebrows & Microblading

Eyebrows & Microblading frame the face, and a good brow can do more for a face than most people expect. Microblading places fine strokes of pigment into the skin to fill sparse brows and shape them to the face, so mornings get shorter and the mirror gets kinder. The result lasts a year or more with a touch-up, and the shape is planned with the face at rest, not with a pencil line drawn in a rush. For someone already thinking about Botox around the brows, the two are worth planning together, because a brow lift from Botox and a brow shape from microblading can either work together or fight each other.

How to choose the right care

Start with the change you want to see in the mirror, then ask what causes it. A line that appears when you frown is a muscle problem, and Botox is the tool for it. A spot that is there whether you move or not is a skin problem, and a laser or a peel is the tool for that. A hollow cheek is a volume problem, and a filler is the tool. A clinic that listens will sort the three out before it quotes a price, and it will tell you when the answer is to wait. Bring a photo of yourself from five years ago if you have one; it helps the provider see what has changed and what has stayed the same, and it keeps the plan aimed at you rather than at a trend.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I expect at my first Botox visit?

A talk first, then the treatment. The provider will ask what bothers you, watch your face move, and map out the dose before any needle comes out. The injections take about fifteen minutes, and you can go back to your day right after.

How do I know how many units I need?

It depends on the size of the muscles and the areas you want treated. Strong frown lines need more than fine forehead lines. A fair clinic quotes the price per unit and the total for your plan before the visit starts.

Can Botox fix lines that are there all the time?

Partly. Lines that appear when you move respond best, because Botox relaxes the muscle that makes them. Lines that stay put at rest are set into the skin, and they soften over time with repeat visits, often helped by a laser or a peel.

Is there downtime after Botox?

Almost none. You may have small marks where the needle went in, and they fade within hours. The usual advice is to stay upright for a few hours, skip heavy exercise for a day, and leave the treated spots alone.

Does Botox work for jaw pain or grinding?

It can help. Botox in the jaw muscles eases the clenching that causes headaches and worn teeth for some people. It is a medical use, so it needs a different plan and a provider who knows the anatomy well.

What is the difference between Botox and Dysport?

Both relax muscles, and both are made from the same purified protein. Dysport spreads a little wider and may show a touch faster, while Botox stays more where it is placed. The right one depends on the area and the provider's habit, not on brand loyalty.

When should I book before an event?

At least two weeks ahead. The result takes that long to settle, and it gives time for a small adjustment if one side needs a little more. Booking the week of the event leaves no room to fix anything.
